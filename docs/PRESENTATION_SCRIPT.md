# Presentation script

Use the seven slides as the main presentation. Rehearse aloud to stay within the course's seven-minute limit. State the complete request within the first 30 seconds and repeat it at closing. Orient the audience to each chart before interpreting it. All data are synthetic training data.

## Slide 1 — Funding request

We ask you to approve a 40-million-riyal envelope for Najran, Jazan and Northern Borders. Authorize ten million for pilots only after approval of the metric, baseline, comparison design, service plans and costs; hold thirty million for review. We recommend targeting regions with substantial gaps and activity, then testing before scaling. The stakes are regional gaps against the 65-percent course target.

## Slide 2 — National situation

The horizontal axis runs from January 2022 to December 2025. The vertical axis shows the course adoption index, from zero to one hundred percent. The teal line is the monthly arithmetic mean; the dashed orange line is the 65-percent target. Adoption increased from 51.40 to 63.33 percent, a rise of 11.93 percentage points. December remains 1.67 points below target.

This is the mean of the supplied adoption percentages, following the course labs. It is not a directly observed digital-transactions share. A transaction-weighted alternative gives 66.12 percent, above target. We disclose that sensitivity and use the course definition consistently. The latest national average also hides regional differences.

## Slide 3 — Regional complication

Each horizontal bar shows a region's December 2025 arithmetic-mean adoption. Longer bars mean higher adoption. The orange reference marks 65 percent, and teal highlights the proposed pilot regions. Nine of thirteen regions remain below target. Najran is lowest at 58.89 percent. Al-Baha, Jazan and Northern Borders also have substantial gaps.

We need a transparent rule for deciding where the first funding should go. Choosing only the lowest percentage would overlook how much activity each region handles.

## Slide 4 — Priority evidence and its limits

Each bar ranks one of the nine lagging regions in December 2025. The horizontal axis runs from zero to six hundred priority-index units. Our index is the positive gap to 65 divided by one hundred, multiplied by December transactions. Teal highlights the proposed pilot regions; orange highlights Al-Baha. Longer bars mean greater priority under this rule. It balances shortfall with activity; it does not estimate converted transactions or return on investment.

Najran combines a 6.11-point gap with 9,329 transactions. Jazan has a 4.34-point gap and 10,490 transactions. Northern Borders has a 4.09-point gap and 10,917 transactions. These three represent 42.61 percent of the priority index across lagging regions. That percentage refers to the index, not users or transactions.

Third place is a near tie: Northern Borders scores 446.26 and Al-Baha 445.60, a difference of only 0.66 index units. Weighting adoption by transactions selects Al-Baha instead of Jazan. Before any pilot release, agree the definition and validate demand; a changed definition requires reranking and reallocating. The data cannot establish the cause of low adoption or which intervention works best. The annotated chart uses the source CSV and workbook field definitions; it is not an actual Tableau-export screenshot.

## Slide 5 — Allocation options

We compare three policies. Equal funding divides forty million by nine, approximately 4.44 million each, but overlooks different gaps and activity. Full concentration commits forty million to three priorities before testing. Staged funding reserves thirty-five million for the regions and five million for reassessment.

We recommend staged funding: release ten million after baseline approval and hold thirty million for review. The dataset does not establish causal returns for any option.

## Slide 6 — Recommendation and conditional scenario

The proposed regional envelopes are thirteen million for Najran and eleven million each for Jazan and Northern Borders. Five million supports evaluation and reassessment. We distribute the regional thirty-five million in proportion to the priority scores and round to whole millions using largest remainders. These are envelopes, not verified implementation costs. Candidate interventions include assisted onboarding and testing friction in high-volume services; local diagnosis must establish the actual plan.

The 64.45-percent figure is a conditional scenario. If all three regions reach 65 percent and every other region stays unchanged, the national course index reaches 64.45 percent. It still falls short of the national target. This arithmetic does not predict what forty million will achieve. It makes the limit of the first wave visible.

## Slide 7 — Closing request and next step

Approve forty million and authorize ten million only after baseline approval. Establish an accountable implementation owner at approval. Before any release or intervention, agree the metric, baseline, comparison, service plan and cost; validate the shortlist, then launch within thirty days if ready.

At day ninety from envelope approval, review impact and cost. Report the pilot's actual duration; extend the review if exposure is insufficient. The proposed goal is two percentage points per pilot, with stable satisfaction and service performance. This is a management goal, not a prediction. If evidence is weak, pause later releases and revise the plan.
