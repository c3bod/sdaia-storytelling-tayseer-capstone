# Questions and answers

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Why invest after meeting the national target? | The 66.21% national result hides eight regions still below 65%. |
| Why highlight Al-Baha? | Its weighted adoption is 62.78%, below target. All eight laggards are highlighted. |
| What does the orange line mean? | The 65% target. |
| Why Najran, Northern Borders, Al-Baha and Jazan? | They have the four widest positive target gaps. |
| What does 87% mean? | Their combined gaps divided by the combined positive gaps of all eight laggards, about 86.96%. |
| How is adoption calculated? | SUM(adoption percentage × user weight) / SUM(user weight), matching the supplied Tableau reference. |
| Why not average regional percentages? | Regional user weights differ. Compute the national measure from underlying rows. |
| How are budgets rounded? | Half-million units using largest remainders, preserving the 40M total. |
| Where does 5M go? | Asir 2M, Tabuk 1.5M, Hail 1M and Al-Jouf 0.5M. |
| Why 30 days? | Proposed time to validate local service plans and costs before spending. |
| Why six months? | Proposed checkpoint for adoption, spending and service quality, followed by adjustments. |
| What increase will 40M produce? | The data do not establish a reliable causal forecast. The objective is to move each region toward 65%. |
| Real service results? | No, synthetic training data. |
| Can user counts be summed as population? | No. People may overlap across services/channels; these are weights. |
| What is the evidence? | Raw CSV, independently executed notebook, matched charts and budget table, plus Tableau. The public reference is the December snapshot; our trend uses the full CSV. |
